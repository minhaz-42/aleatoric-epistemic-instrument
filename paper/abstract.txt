Escalating a query to an expensive model pays off only when the cheap model’s error is reducible, so uncertainty-aware routers decompose predictive uncertainty and route on the reducible part. Two 2026 results establish the pieces this rests on: the mutual-information estimate of epistemic uncertainty is not the reducible kind, and the irreducible term cancels from the escalation decision. Both assume the estimator one would like — independently seeded ensemble members, or multiple independent annotations per input. We ask what the estimator you can actually build does instead, in a synthetic video-forecasting world where the true conditional $P^*$ is exact in closed form, so every estimate can be compared against the quantity it claims to estimate. (i) As members go from independent to identical at fixed per-member error, the ensemble’s true error rises 10.0× while its estimate falls to zero; they cross near ρ = 0.5, and the estimator’s advantage over random routing is gone by ρ = 0.75. (ii) Whether confidence routing decays or inverts below random is decided by why the cheap model is worse: benign under missing evidence, catastrophic under miscalibration, floor-invariant where the estimate is well-posed. (iii) Video has a second lever, and 92%–98% of the gain from extending the observed prefix is exactly the term that cancels from model escalation. Everything reproduces over 5 seeds and two structurally different worlds. No vision-language model was run, and nothing here is evidence about real video.
